# What Being a CR Really Means: A Senior's Take

## The Core Responsibility: You're the Communication Bridge

The main thing we do in our branch is we are the connection link between the department and our class. Whatever information the HOD or faculty members want to communicate, we are responsible for sharing it with the entire class.

Another responsibility is to contact the respective faculty members according to their allotted timetable and inform or call them when required.

**That's it.** Apart from these, we haven't really had many other responsibilities as CRs in our EEE department. I think it will be mostly the same in CSE as well.

---

## Myth vs Reality

### The Scary Stories (Don't Believe Them)

People may scare you saying being a CR is risky, but honestly **it's not that big of a deal**. It's mainly just about communication only.

The real challenge isn't the role itself—it's coordination.

---

## The Hidden Challenge: Coordination with Your Co-CR

When you are a CR, your co-CR has to be responsible as well. If you guys don't have coordination it gets to your nerves and you burnout.

### How We Handle It

Me and our co-CR are like, **1 whole semester he'll single handedly handle, next sem I'll handle**. There's no coordination. It's just division. 

- Some sems are more stressful
- Some sems are less stressful
- **This one is eating me up** (not sad though, it's fun)

---

## What This Role Actually Does to You

**It's breaking me everyday. I'm rebuilding it bit by bit.**

That's why this CR thing is about—it makes you:

- **More confident** → You communicate with faculty, HOD, entire class
- **Responsible** → People depend on you for critical information
- **Resilient** → You handle stress, learn to divide work, adapt each semester

The role isn't about grand leadership. It's about showing up, communicating clearly, and handling the weight when it comes.

---

## Bottom Line

Being a CR is a grind, but it's the *good kind of grind*. You'll grow in ways you don't expect.
